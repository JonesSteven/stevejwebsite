Open-source AI projects

Hands-on experiments with frontier models, built in the open.

Android · Kotlin

SlothSpeak

The convenience of voice without the anthropomorphization: no persona, no fake friendship, no simulated small talk. This bring-your-own-keys mobile app runs the most capable models from OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and xAI at maximum reasoning effort, takes minutes when it needs to, and reads back a thorough, sourced answer. It includes hands-free follow-ups and deep research, with no server backend.

Next.js · TypeScript

Jev Civilizations

Built to demonstrate the capabilities of decision models using the Jev API. In this strategy game, an AI model decides the actions of four autonomous tribes while you can only shape their environment. A deterministic game engine works out the consequences, and a decision inspector shows exactly what the model was told.

Next.js · Supabase

Image Iguana

A self-hosted studio for Google’s Gemini image models, with multi-turn conversational editing, reference images, parallel generation, a prompt library and a usage dashboard that tracks estimated spend.

TypeScript · Email agent

StudentSafeAI

A supervised research assistant that students use by email. The AI writes an age-appropriate, cited research essay, and a parent must approve it before the student receives it.

I talked about SlothSpeak, StudentSafeAI and building AI products that deliver value through intelligence rather than manipulation in All Juice, No Pulp at the Comox Valley AI meetup (BC + AI), May 2026. Read the recap →

Off the clock

University shenanigans

Before the boardroom, I spent my Engineering Physics years at UBC building improbable machines for international competitions.

NASA Centennial Challenge · 2005

Racing a robot up a space elevator

I captained UBC Snowstar, a 20-member engineering team, in the Space Elevator Games, the first of NASA’s Centennial Challenges. Our 7 kg robot climbed a ribbon powered only by a 10,000-watt searchlight shining on its solar panels. The judges named us the team most likely to win the next year, and the project landed in The New York Times and on CNN.

Read the New York Times story →

DARPA Grand Challenge · 2005

Teaching a car to drive itself

Long before self-driving cars were a product category, I was part of UBC’s student team building an autonomous vehicle for DARPA’s Grand Challenge, the driverless race across the desert. Discovery Channel Canada featured the project.

Watch the segment on YouTube →

Red Bull Flugtag · 2006

Flying (briefly) off a pier

At Canada’s first Red Bull Flugtag in Vancouver, teams launch homemade, human-powered flying machines off a pier and into the ocean. Most of them fly about as well as you’d expect.
