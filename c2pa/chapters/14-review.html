Read it from the bottom up, as a validator does: is the time trustworthy, is the signer trusted, does the signature cover the claim, does each assertion match its hash, do the content bytes match the hard binding? Then, for CAWG: does the person’s signature cover the hard binding, and do you trust their credential?

Where to go next

  • C2PA Technical Specification: spec.c2pa.org, especially the Validation and Trust Model sections.
  • CAWG specifications: cawg.io, for the identity, training-and-data-mining and metadata assertions, and current drafts.
  • Open-source tools: opensource.contentauthenticity.org (c2patool, c2pa-rs, c2pa-js, Python bindings).
  • Public test files: github.com/c2pa-org/public-testfiles, for more good and deliberately broken samples.
  • Cryptography, deeper: RFC 5280 (X.509 path validation), RFC 9052 (COSE), RFC 3161 (time-stamps), RFC 8949 (CBOR).

You started with “why can’t I trust a photo’s metadata?”. You can now explain, link by link, how a signature from a camera you’ve never met can tell you something trustworthy about a picture, and exactly where that trust ends.
